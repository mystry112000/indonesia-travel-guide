Sumatra

The Wild Frontier

Overview

Untamed Wilderness

Sumatra is Indonesia's wildest island — home to orangutans, tigers, elephants, and the world's largest volcanic lake. It offers raw adventure: jungle trekking, world-class surf, and the legendary cuisine of Padang. Less developed than Bali or Java, Sumatra rewards travellers who crave authenticity and nature.

  • Population: 59 million
  • Largest city: Medan (2.1 million)
  • Main airport: Kuala Namu (KNO), Medan
  • Key attractions: Orangutans, Lake Toba, Mentawai surf, Padang food
  • Best for: Wildlife, adventure, culture, surfing

Top Attractions

Must-See in Sumatra

Bukit Lawang — Orangutan Jungle

Trek through Gunung Leuser rainforest to see wild orangutans. 2-day/1-night treks include river tubing back to town.

Lake Toba & Samosir Island

The world's largest volcanic lake. Relax on Samosir Island, visit Batak villages, and learn about the unique Batak culture.

Rafflesia Bloom

See the world's largest flower (up to 1m diameter) in Bengkulu or Kerinci. Bloom season: Nov-Jan, unpredictable timing.

Padang & Minangkabau Culture

Capital of Indonesian cuisine. Eat Nasi Padang, visit the stunning Rumah Gadang traditional houses in Bukittinggi.

Harau Valley

Dramatic granite cliffs, waterfalls, and rice paddies near Payakumbuh. A quieter alternative to Bali's rice terraces.

Mentawai Islands

World-class surfing with consistent swell year-round. Remote, pristine beaches. Boat charters from Padang.

Local Tip

Best Time to Visit

  • Dry season (Apr-Sep): Best jungle trekking conditions in Bukit Lawang and Gunung Leuser.
  • Wet season (Oct-Mar): Heavier rain but cheaper and quieter. Some trekking trails may close.
  • Surf season: Mentawai best waves May-Oct (dry) and Nov-Apr (larger swells).
  • Lake Toba: Year-round destination but Dec-Feb can be chilly (18°C nights).
  • Festivals: Pacu Jawi (cow race, Aug-Oct), Tabuik (Ashura, Jan).
Travel Tips

Sumatra Essentials

Getting Around

Public transport: buses and minibuses between cities. Medan to Bukit Lawang: 3h by shared minibus ($5). Rent a 4x4 for remote areas.

Padang Food Rules

In Padang restaurants, food is served on stacked plates. Only pay for what you eat. Nasi Padang is famously spicy — say "kurang pedas" for less chili.

Wildlife Safety

Keep at least 10m from orangutans. Don't touch them — they can transmit diseases. Always follow your guide's instructions.

Culture

Sumatra's Tribes

Sumatra is home to dozens of ethnic groups, each with distinct languages, architecture, and traditions.

Batak (North Sumatra)

Ancient animist traditions blended with Christianity. Famous for their distinctive curved-roof houses (Rumah Bolon), polyphonic choir singing, and the spicy Andaliman pepper used in their cuisine.

Minangkabau (West Sumatra)

The world's largest matrilineal society. Property and family name pass through the mother. Known for Rumah Gadang (buffalo-horn roofs), Randang, and a strong Islamic identity.

Mentawai (Mentawai Islands)

One of Indonesia's oldest indigenous cultures. Known for full-body tattoos (pre-dating European contact), sharpened teeth, and animist shamanism. Their lifestyle remains largely traditional in remote areas.

Food

Sumatran Cuisine

Sumatra is the culinary heart of Indonesia — bold, spicy, and coconut-rich. Padang food has conquered the entire archipelago.

Rendang — World's Best Food

Beef slow-cooked for hours in coconut milk, lemongrass, and chilies until caramelized. CNN named it the #1 World's Most Delicious Food in 2017 & 2024.

Nasi Padang

Steamed rice surrounded by small plates of dishes: rendang, ayam pop, sambal hijau, gulai, and more. You only pay for what you eat from the stack.

Sate Padang & Lemang

Sate Padang: beef offal skewers in thick turmeric sauce. Lemang: glutinous rice cooked inside bamboo tubes, served with rendang during festivities.

History

Sumatra's Story

  • 7th C — Srivijaya Empire (Palembang) becomes a Buddhist thalassocracy controlling the Malacca Strait
  • 1292 — Marco Polo visits north Sumatra on his return from China
  • 1496 — Aceh Sultanate founded; becomes a major Islamic power
  • 1873-1904 — Aceh War: Dutch fought the bloodiest colonial war in the Indies (60,000+ dead)
  • 1883 — Krakatoa eruption: the loudest sound in history, felt across Sumatra
  • 1909 — First orangutan conservation in Sumatra established
  • 2004 — Indian Ocean tsunami kills 170,000 in Aceh; triggers peace agreement ending 30-year insurgency
  • 2014 — Gunung Sinabung eruptions destroy villages; 16 killed
  • Today — Sumatra's rainforests halved since 1980; conservation critical

Customs

Local Etiquette

Dress Modestly in Aceh

Aceh follows Sharia law. Women should cover shoulders and knees. Men should wear long pants in villages. Swimwear is fine at tourist resorts only.

Right Hand Only

Always give and receive with your right hand. The left hand is considered unclean. When eating with hands (common in Padang), use only your right.

Respect the Forest

Don't litter in the jungle. Don't touch or feed wildlife. Many Batak and Mentawai communities consider certain forest areas sacred.

Beyond Sumatra

Extend your adventure with these nearby destinations accessible from Sumatra.

Pulau Weh

Indonesia's northernmost island, 45min ferry from Banda Aceh. World-class snorkelling with manta rays, orca sightings, and pristine coral reefs. Budget: $20-35/day.

Banyak Islands

Remote surfing paradise off Aceh's west coast. Consistent waves, empty beaches, and affordable homestays. 8h from Medan by road + fast boat.

Peninsular Malaysia

Ferry from Dumai (Riau) to Malacca or Medan to Penang. Explore George Town's street art, eat world-famous street food, and experience Malaysia's multicultural vibe.
