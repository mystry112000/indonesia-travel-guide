Overview

Welcome to Paradise

Bali is Indonesia's most famous island — a lush volcanic landscape of rice terraces, ancient temples, and surf breaks. Known as the "Island of the Gods," it blends Hindu traditions with world-class tourism infrastructure. Whether you want surf, culture, nightlife, or wellness — Bali delivers.

  • Population: 4.3 million
  • Religion: Balinese Hinduism (95%)
  • Main airport: Ngurah Rai (DPS)
  • Currency: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR)
  • Best for: Surfing, temples, yoga, nightlife

Top Attractions

Must-See in Bali

Tegalalang Rice Terraces

Iconic stepped rice paddies in Ubud. Best visited at sunrise before the crowds arrive. Free to walk through.

Uluwatu Temple

Clifftop sea temple with stunning sunset views. Don't miss the nightly Kecak fire dance performance.

Mount Batur Sunrise Trek

Hike an active volcano before dawn to watch the sunrise above the clouds. Guided tours from $35.

Local Tip

Best Time to Visit

  • Dry season (Apr-Sep): Best weather, clear skies, great for trekking and beach. Peak tourism Jul-Aug.
  • Wet season (Oct-Mar): Rainy afternoons, fewer crowds, lush green landscapes. Best surf waves Nov-Feb.
  • Shoulder months (Apr-Jun, Sep): Perfect balance — good weather, fewer tourists.
  • Nyepi (March): Bali's Day of Silence — everything closed, no flights. Unique cultural experience.
  • Galungan: Balinese Hindu festival every 210 days — colourful celebrations island-wide.
Travel Tips

Bali Essentials

Getting Around

Rent a scooter ($5-8/day) for freedom. Go-Jek app for cheap rides. Taxis from airport to Kuta: $10-15.

Temple Etiquette

Cover shoulders & knees. Wear a sarong (rental at temples). Don't point feet at altars. Women on period cannot enter.

Money

ATMs everywhere but carry cash for small shops. Credit cards accepted at hotels and larger restaurants.

Culture

Ceremonies & Traditions

Bali's Hindu culture is woven into daily life. There are over 50 major ceremonies each year.

Nyepi — Day of Silence

Bali's Hindu New Year. Everything closes including the airport. The night before, massive Ogoh-Ogoh demon statues are paraded. A once-in-a-lifetime experience every March.

Galungan & Kuningan

Every 210 days, Balinese Hinduism celebrates the victory of dharma over adharma. Penjor (bamboo poles) line the streets, and families pray at temples.

Odalan — Temple Festivals

Each of Bali's 20,000+ temples celebrates its anniversary every 210 days. Expect dance performances, food offerings, and crowds in traditional dress.

Food

Balinese Cuisine

Unlike the rest of Indonesia, Balinese food is distinct — rich with turmeric, galangal, and shrimp paste.

Nasi Goreng

Indonesia's national dish — fried rice with kecap manis, shallots, and egg. Bali's version often adds sambal matah on top.

Babi Guling

Bali's most famous dish — spit-roasted suckling pig stuffed with turmeric, lemongrass, and chili. Must-try at Ibu Oka in Ubud.

Lawar & Sate Lilit

Lawar is a minced meat salad with coconut and herbs. Sate Lilit is spiced minced seafood wrapped around lemongrass sticks.

History

Bali's Past

  • 914 AD — First written records of Balinese kingdom
  • 1343 — Majapahit Empire conquers Bali, bringing Hindu-Javanese culture
  • 1520 — Bali becomes independent as Majapahit falls
  • 1597 — First Dutch ship arrives; Bali resists colonization for 300 years
  • 1846-1908 — Dutch wars of conquest; Balinese royals perform mass ritual suicide (Puputan)
  • 1930s — Western tourists discover Bali's culture and beaches
  • 1963 — Mount Agung eruption kills 1,500
  • 2002 & 2005 — Kuta bombings transform tourism industry
  • Today — 6+ million tourists visit annually
FAQ

Bali FAQs

Bali is affordable. Budget travellers: $30-40/day. Mid-range: $60-100/day. Luxury: $150-500/day. The exchange rate is extremely favourable for USD, EUR, and AUD.

Many nationalities get visa-free entry for 30 days (not extendable) or Visa on Arrival ($35, 30 days, extendable once). Check with the Indonesian embassy for your country.

Rent a scooter ($5-8/day) — most popular option. Go-Jek/Grab motorbike taxis for short trips. Private driver: $35-50/day. Taxis are metered in South Bali only.

Customs

Balinese Etiquette

Respect Temple Dress Code

Always wear a sarong and sash when entering temples. Rentals are usually available at the entrance. Women menstruating should not enter temples according to local belief.

Don't Touch Offerings

Small canang sari offerings are placed everywhere — on sidewalks, in shops, even on motorbikes. Never step on or touch them. They are sacred gifts to the gods.

Polite Behaviour

Never touch anyone's head (it's spiritually sacred). Use your right hand for giving/receiving. Don't point with your finger — use your thumb instead. Speak softly and smile.

Nearby Islands

Bali is the perfect base for island-hopping. These destinations are a short trip away.

Nusa Penida

40min fast boat from Sanur. Home to Kelingking Beach (T-Rex cliff), Crystal Bay, and Angel's Billabong. Rent a scooter to explore — roads are rough but the views are unreal.

Gili Islands

2h from Padang Bai. Three car-free islands: Gili Trawangan (party), Gili Meno (honeymoon), Gili Air (balanced). World-class snorkelling with turtles and underwater statues.

Lombok

Bali's quieter neighbour. Climb Mount Rinjani (3,726m), relax on Senggigi Beach, or surf at Desert Point. Fast boat from Padang Bai: $20-30.
